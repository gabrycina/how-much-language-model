Method note: clean-room reimplementation from the cached scores (seed-0 split

## W4 — Holm correction (7 arms vs no rescoring)

After Holm step-down correction at 0.05, only Jev remains significant
(Holm-adjusted p = 0.042). OPT-6.7b, the strongest per-candidate arm
(raw p = 0.035), does not survive (adjusted p = 0.21). Adjusted p-values:
GPT-2 large 0.25, Qwen2.5-7B 0.256, GPT-2 medium 0.256, GPT-2 small 0.63,
DistilGPT-2 0.63. Recomputation from cached scores was attempted (paired
bootstrap, 5000 resamples, test only, recorded alphas) but the paper's WER
recipe proved unrecoverable and Jev's typed pick is not in the cached scores,
so the correction is applied to the reported p-values.

## W8 — Alpha-tuning stability (419-sentence dev)

The tuned interpolation weight is unstable but harmless. Across 200 bootstrap
resamples of dev, the chosen alpha spreads over ~10 grid values per arm
(std 0.10–0.16; e.g. OPT mode 0.25 with 85/200, range 0.0–0.55), yet the
resulting test WER is stable (std 0.10–0.21pp; OPT 8.88 ± 0.21, range
8.68–9.39). The WER surface is flat near the optimum: tuning on 419 sentences
jitters the weight without overfitting the outcome. (Refits land one grid step
from the recorded alphas, consistent with the flat optimum and the WER-recipe
difference noted above.)

## Q6 — z-normalization

(a) Candidate counts are heavily skewed: mean 38.7, median 15, min 1, max 100;
42.7% of the 1397 lists have fewer than 10 candidates (29.9% fewer than 5;
81 single-candidate lists). (b) Replacing per-list z-normalization with one
global mean/std (estimated on dev, alpha retuned) hurts every arm by +0.08 to
+0.42pp test WER, and dev tuning then discards the LM entirely (retuned alpha
0.00–0.05; four of six arms choose no rescoring). Per-list normalization is
load-bearing: a single global scale cannot align LM log-likelihoods across
lists. The arm ranking shuffles (OPT becomes best) but no new significance
claim emerges. (c) No pathology on short lists: on the 377 test lists with
<10 candidates, the per-list z-norm top-1 agrees with a raw-score combination
on 99.5% of lists, and short-list WER is ~0.5% under either rule.

## Q7 — Calibration vs ranking for abstention

(a) Isotonic recalibration of the temperature-scaled confidences introduces
zero rank inversions (0 of 477,753 sentence pairs) and leaves the
coverage–risk curve exactly unchanged: the gap to Jev is not a calibration
problem. (b) Among per-candidate confidence signals, max softmax probability
(the current baseline) beats the top-1–top-2 combined-score margin, while the
raw top-1 combined score is anti-informative — sentence error rises as coverage
falls, because per-list z-scoring makes raw top-1 values incomparable across
lists. No per-candidate signal closes the gap, and the baseline already uses
the best one. The decisive evidence is in the reported curves: at 100%
coverage OPT and Jev have identical sentence error (28.4% vs 28.7%), yet Jev's
curve falls far faster (3.4% vs 7.5% at 30% coverage). Same top-1 accuracy,
better ordering of kept sentences — Jev's abstention advantage is a ranking
advantage (its confidences separate right from wrong: 0.736 vs 0.409, gap 0.327,
against OPT's 0.647 vs 0.368, gap 0.279), not calibration.
