Q2 sensitivity: pruning Kaldi 100-best lists to top-20 (decoder order)
================================================================
Split: first 419 lists dev, last 978 test (insertion order). Alpha re-tuned
per arm on pruned dev (grid 0.00-1.00, step 0.05, min dev WER, first-min
tie-break). Paired bootstrap vs no-rescoring on pruned test (2000 resamples,
one-sided, seed 0). The hosted Jev typed arm could not be re-run on pruned
lists; all arm claims below cover per-candidate arms only.

Ceiling numbers (test)
- Published N=100: oracle WER 2.52%, truth-in-list 89.37%.
- Pruned N=20 (same reimplementation): oracle WER 4.24%, truth-in-list 82.72%.
- Within-implementation N=100 reference: oracle 3.08%, truth-in-list 86.81%.
  (Exact replication of the published pipeline was not possible; the N=20 vs
  N=100 deltas below are computed within one consistent reimplementation.)
- Paper sentence: "Pruning the candidate set from 100 to the top-20 moves
  oracle WER from 2.5% to 4.2% and truth-in-list from 89% to 83%; these bound
  what any selection method, typed or per-candidate, can achieve at N=20."

Rescoring at N=20 (test WER; tuned dev alpha in brackets)
- No rescoring: 9.39% (unchanged by pruning: the top-1 hypothesis always
  survives a top-20 cut).
- OPT-6.7b: 8.88% (a=0.30), gain 0.51pp, p=0.0005.
- Qwen2.5-7B: 8.83% (a=0.35), gain 0.56pp, p=0.0015.
- GPT-2 large: 9.09% (a=0.35), gain 0.30pp, p=0.035.
- GPT-2 small: 9.39% (a=0.00, dev optimum; gain 0.00pp, n.s.).
- DistilGPT-2: 9.39% (a=0.00, dev optimum; gain 0.00pp, n.s.).

Efficiency finding
- The 93x parameter range (82M-7.6B) stays flat: max-min WER across
  per-candidate arms is 0.56pp at N=20 vs 0.60pp at N=100 (within
  implementation; published N=100 spread was 0.35pp). No arm dominates:
  best arm is Qwen at N=20 (8.83%) vs OPT at N=100 (8.79%), 0.05pp apart.
- Significance pattern persists: the same three large arms (OPT, Qwen, GPT-2)
  are significant vs no-rescoring at both N=100 and N=20; the two small
  models show no significant gain at either N.
- Paper sentence: "At N=20 the per-candidate spread across the 93x parameter
  range is 0.6pp, essentially unchanged from N=100 (0.6pp); OPT, Qwen and
  GPT-2 remain the only significant arms (paired bootstrap, p<0.05), so the
  efficiency conclusion does not depend on candidate-set size."

Gain retention (within-implementation, N=20 gain / N=100 gain)
- OPT: 0.51/0.60 = 85% retained. Qwen: 0.56/0.59 = 95% retained.
  GPT-2: 0.30/0.47 = 65% retained.
- Paper sentence: "Pruning to top-20 retains 65-95% of each arm's N=100
  rescoring gain (OPT 85%, Qwen 95%, GPT-2 65%); absolute gains are similar
  or slightly smaller, and no arm's conclusion flips."

Caveats
- Small-model zeros are tie-break artefacts: gpt2small (N=100) and
  distilgpt2 (N=20) had dev-tied alphas including 0. Alternative tied alphas
  give small test gains (gpt2small N=100 a=0.2 -> 9.00%, +0.39pp;
  distilgpt2 N=20 a=0.25 -> 9.22%, +0.17pp), still below big-model gains.
- Only 45.8% of lists have >=20 candidates, so pruning binds on a minority
  of lists; the first-pass baseline is unaffected by construction.
